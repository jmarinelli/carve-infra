The secondary vehicle market has a structural problem. Service history exists — it is simply unstructured, unverifiable, and invisible at the moment it matters most.

What this is about

A verification layer built from the service level up — not assembled from secondary sources, but constructed forward, event by event.

Workshops sign service events. Vehicles accumulate verified history. The result is a primary record: immutable, portable, structurally credible.

The goal: a market where verified history is a standard input in pricing, underwriting, and risk — not an exception.

02
The ProblemMarket Structure02 / 09

Buyers make high-value decisions using information they cannot verify. The market rewards opacity and penalizes honesty.

01
History exists but can't be trusted

Service records are fragmented across paper, workshop systems, and WhatsApp. Non-standardized, non-portable, and trivially easy to fabricate or omit.

Akerlof, 1970 — "Market for Lemons": sellers know more than buyers; the market prices all vehicles as if they might be bad.

02
Existing solutions are retrospective

History report providers aggregate what's already recorded in registries. They create no new signal — bounded by institutional data that wasn't designed for this purpose.

Point-in-time inspections are useful. They cannot substitute for a documented longitudinal history of consistent maintenance.

03
Quality is invisible at point of sale

Well-maintained vehicles sell at the same price as neglected ones. Honest sellers are systematically underrewarded. The market selects against quality — exactly as Akerlof predicted.

The lemons problem is not theoretical. It is the operating condition of the secondary vehicle market today.

03
The InsightCore Thesis03 / 09

Trust must be built forward,
not reconstructed backward.

01
Where real information is created

The service layer — workshops, inspection centers, maintenance providers — is where information about actual vehicle condition is produced. Every repair, oil change, and measurement is a verifiable event with diagnostic significance.

02
No new behavior required

Workshops already perform services. Owners already maintain vehicles. The raw material for verification is produced continuously — it is simply unstructured and unverifiable. The challenge is infrastructure, not habit change.

03
A primary record, not an aggregation

Signed at the moment of execution, by the actor with direct knowledge of the vehicle's state. Not assembled from registries. Not retrospective. A record that persists across every ownership change — becoming more valuable over time.

The Adoption ModelSupply First05 / 09

The classic circular dependency: workshops won't sign if buyers don't care, buyers won't care if there's no signal. The model breaks it by ensuring workshops generate value for themselves before buyers care.

1

Workshops adopt for operational value — structured records, dispute protection, client certificates. No market recognition needed.

2

Vehicles accumulate history passively as a byproduct of workshop activity, even before owners claim them.

3

Owners discover their vehicle already has verified history. Passive accumulation converts inertia into engagement.

4

The Official Report travels into listings and negotiations — normalizing the signal without platform-wide adoption.

5

Buyer awareness grows, creating demand-side pull. Institutional actors develop appetite for programmatic access.

How the Signal WorksVerification Coverage06 / 09

Verification Coverage is not a score. It is a composite measure of how much of a vehicle's operational history has been validated by verified nodes — and how structurally coherent that history is.

Continuity

How much of the vehicle's mileage is accounted for by signed events, without unexplained gaps. Not quantity of events — coherent progression.

Depth

What proportion of the vehicle's operational life is documented. A vehicle with 8 years of history and 6 verified is more legible than one with only episodic records.

Integrity

Whether the event timeline is structurally consistent — no odometer contradictions, no implausible sequences. Inconsistencies are recorded, not hidden.

Coverage cannot be manufactured retroactively. It can only grow through genuine, forward-time node-signed events. That is what makes it a durable signal.

What the signal does not claim

It does not certify a vehicle is defect-free. It represents what has been documented, independently signed, and how consistent and continuous that record is.

Why Now — and Why LATAM FirstMarket Timing07 / 09
Conditions more favorable now than ever

Workshop digitization is reaching critical mass — structured event capture is lower-friction than five years ago.

Consumer expectations around provenance are shifting across categories. Receptivity to trust signal products is growing.

Institutional appetite for structured vehicle lifecycle data is real and growing — in underwriting, fleet, and lending.

Secondary market volume is expanding. As high-value transactions grow, the cost of information asymmetry becomes harder to ignore.

The absence of a solution is not for lack of market demand. It is for lack of infrastructure built the right way.

The LATAM opportunity

Where nothing is verified, credible verification carries higher relative value.

High informality is counterintuitively an advantage. The wedge is quality-conscious, digitally capable workshops where documentation has immediate operational value.

Informality premium

Credible history differentiates more sharply where the baseline is near zero.

Platform tailwinds

Rapidly growing digital marketplace adoption creates distribution channels that didn't exist a few years ago.

Deliberate wedge

Not the whole market — a narrow, high-visibility segment that proves value before broad rollout.
